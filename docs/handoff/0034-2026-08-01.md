## 2. The screening contract now refuses to outrun its evidence (#96, ADR-0072)

One production parcel showed both faults on one screen: **"Strong candidate — projected margin of 55% and no elevated risk signal"**, sitting directly above three risk flags and "No comps — not enough recorded comparable sales nearby yet".

Both were produced correctly by the rules. Both read wrongly.

**The margin rested on a stand-in.** `phl:882917721` had no AVM estimate at all, so the deal model fell back to the OPA assessed value for acquisition — the 55% was arithmetic on that substitution. `CRITICAL_FIELDS` covers only buildability, so valuation availability never reached the screening decision. A strong margin whose price side is an assessment is now `worth_investigating` and says so. The parcel is not hidden and the margin is not suppressed; only the confidence claim is withdrawn.

**"No elevated risk signal" was read as "no risk".** True against a threshold of 60 at a score of 30, and sitting above three visible warnings. The clause now names what it stands beside: a score below the threshold, *but* three flags recorded and worth reviewing. With no flags it reads "no risk flags recorded" — affirmative, not a threshold statement.

`decision_version` stays `d1`: no vocabulary, threshold, or rule ordering changed.

### 2.1 The recompute, because the code change alone reached nobody

Only `strong_candidate` rows can change, so the affected set was 14,474 — not the full 551k. Targeted recompute, 27 minutes:

|---|---:|---:|---:|

| all others | — | — | unchanged |

**4,421 Philadelphia parcels had been presenting as strong candidates on a margin priced from an assessment.** Prediction was 4,449; actual 4,421, and no unaffected bucket moved.

There is no make target for a subset recompute. The one-off used here selected `strong_candidate` rows joined to `parcel` for `market_code = 'phl'` and fed them to `recompute_verdicts` in batches of 500 via `docker compose exec -T worker python -`. **`parcel_verdict` has no `market_code`** — join to `parcel`.

**Do not deploy while a recompute is running.** It runs as `docker compose exec` inside `worker`; a deploy runs `compose up -d`, recreates the container, and kills the pass. It is restartable (the query re-selects whatever is still `strong_candidate`) but you lose the elapsed work.

## 3. The Risk tab was blank on two thirds of Philadelphia (#97)

Reported as a black pane on `phl:871600880`. Three defects, all in one screenshot.

**It rendered nothing.** `OwnerPortfolioPanel` showed itself only when `total_count > 1`; the dossier's empty state appeared only when the portfolio was `null`. An owner holding exactly one parcel satisfies neither, so both branches switched off.

Measured: **358,845 of 546,540 Philadelphia parcels with an owner on record — 65.7% — are held by a single-parcel owner.** Not an edge case. Holding one lot is a fact, not an absence, and is now stated.

**The tab named Risk contained no risk.** Its only occupant was the owner portfolio, which its own tooltip calls an assemblage and motivated-seller signal, while risk score and flags lived on the Verdict tab. The reported parcel carries three flags — L&I violations, a zoning overlay, an RCO — and a user tapping **Risk** saw none of them. `RiskPanel` now groups flags by severity under headings that say what the severity means, names each flag's source, and states the count in its summary rather than leaving the reader to weigh a threshold — the same correction ADR-0072 made to the screening reason. A clean lot says what was checked *and that this is not a guarantee*.

Risk was the only tab that could go blank: Land, Docs, Deal, and Verdict each render at least one unconditional component. Checked, not assumed.

**The OSM basemap had no `maxzoom`**, so MapLibre defaulted to 22 and requested tiles OpenStreetMap does not serve above 19. Selecting a parcel flies to `zoom: 19.5`, so *every dossier visit* produced a burst of 400s and a basemap that went blank instead of overzooming. Capped at 19; Google satellite/roadmap keep their own higher limit.

## 4. Process — three things that cost time

**`make check` could not catch a formatting failure that CI enforces.** It ran `ruff check` and `pnpm lint` but neither `ruff format --check` nor `pnpm format:check`, so #97 went green locally and failed CI on prettier. Fixed at the root: **`make fmt-check`** now exists and `make check` depends on it. Use `make check` and it now mirrors the CI web job.

**The e2e suite stayed green through a bug affecting 65.7% of parcels.** No spec had ever opened the Risk tab, and every seeded fixture parcel has a unique owner — so all six would have shown the blank. Two specs now open it. Coverage of files says nothing about coverage of what users touch.

**My first version of the owner assertion depended on a seeded owner being unique**, which is false on a machine that also has the demo dataset loaded (`Jane Smith` collides; `total_count` becomes 2). This is the fixture trap ADR-0071 documents, hit again. The assertion now expresses the invariant — the owner section always says *something* — rather than depending on data the fixture does not own.

**The standing rule keeps earning its place: verify at the layer the user occupies.** Every conclusion this session that mattered came from measuring — the 65.7% from SQL, the blank from a real browser on the reported parcel, the recompute from before/after counts. The API contract alone would have told you the Risk tab was fine.
