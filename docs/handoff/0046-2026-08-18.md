## 4. The three findings that matter most

**Scheduled jobs never received the timeouts they declare.** arq resolves a cron job's timeout from the `cron()` entry, not the `func()` registration. All six long-running jobs carried theirs on the `func()` side only, so each ran on its schedule under the 30-minute worker default. `cron(recompute_verdicts, weekday="sun")` is among them — an ~11.5h job under a 30-minute cap, cancelled every Sunday since it was scheduled. **This is a large part of why v9 and v10 were never swept and why the recompute "never ran".** Fixed in #155 with a drift guard between the two registries.

Found because OPA failed at **04:30:01**, thirty minutes and one second after its 04:00 cron, with the sweep saturating the box. One cancelled dataset ends the entire nightly ingest, because `ingest_market` re-raises `CancelledError` rather than collecting it — only OPA was attempted. **Data is two days stale, not damaged; it retries at 04:00 once the box is free.** Worth confirming it succeeds.

**A gated scoring change shipped early, through data rather than code.** `is_absentee` is unconditional and the batch never touched it; the mailing backfill went in on 08-16 as a "data operation" and the nightly distress cron scored it that night. All 30,119 absentee-vacant parcels already carried their +10 before the sweep — only 5 parcels rose at all. The gate held the code while the data walked in. **A version gate on a derived score only holds if its inputs are gated too.**

**The predicted risk movement was one of three paths.** `verify` does not merely reword the delinquency flag — it halves the weight of any `high` delinquency, and `verify` is reached by two populations the delta never counted. Citywide risk drops project to ~15,547, not 672. Full decomposition in the ledger. **One judgement needs an owner's eye:** ~6,169 parcels have their risk halved on a name mismatch alone, no deed of any kind, against a snapshot frozen in June 2022 — while D3's stated principle is that a parcel-level flag never silently disappears. It does not disappear; it halves.

## 5. Things that will bite the next session

**GitHub Actions is still billing-blocked.** #152, #153, #155 and #156 all merged on local gates alone. Owner action; it is also why branch protection cannot be enforced.

**Never deploy while a sweep is running.** `compose up -d` recreates the worker and kills detached shards.

**Four shards saturate the box.** 0.0% idle, 0.0% iowait, ~19 parcels/s. More shards cannot help; the "~4h with four shards" projection was wrong by ~3x, and the older ~11.7h estimate was the accurate one. Distress, by contrast, sweeps in ~4 minutes.

**Prod latency from a dev machine is ~250ms RTT.** A 0.76s API response is three round-trips over ~16ms of server time. Do not read it as degradation.

**A guard that cannot fail is worse than none.** Two written this session were vacuous on the first attempt — the cron comparison never intersected because `cron()` prefixes `"cron:"`, and a health-board filter compared upstream table names against short registry keys and would have emptied the board entirely. Both were caught only by asserting a control case survived. **Always verify a new guard fails with its fix reverted.**
